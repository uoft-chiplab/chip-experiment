## Weak field-seekers

The magnetic potential of our alkali atoms is

$$U(r) = g_F m_F \mu_B B(r)$$

The conservative force arising from this potential is $F=-\nabla U(r) \propto -\nabla B(r)$. A trapping force therefore requires a positive $U(r)$. Since we can create a local minima of B(r) using a combination of coils and wires, the other necessary ingredient is demanding $g_Fm_F > 0$.  

After our atoms are initially cooled and optically pumped, we therefore target the $\ket{2,2}$ state of Rb-87 ($g_F = 1/2$ and $m_F = 2$ $\implies g_F m_F = 1$) and the $\ket{9/2,9/2}$ state of K-40 ($g_F = 2/9$ and $m_F = 9/2$ $\implies g_Fm_F=1$)

We utilize magnetic trapping in three distinct phases:

- Quadrupole magnetic trapping immediately after optical pumping
- The same trap + the XFER coil to raise atoms towards the chip
- Wires on the chip trap that produce an effective Ioffe-Pritchard trap.

## Levitating against gravity

The B-field gradient must be large enough to balance the force of gravity $F_g = Mg$. Setting $F_g = -\nabla U$ for magnetically trappable K-40 and Rb-87 states, one can show the necessary gradients are at least 7 G/cm and 15 G/cm for K and Rb respectively.
